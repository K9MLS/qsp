    This station

    —

    Who this instance says it is when it links to another network. A blank callsign appears on the far end's dashboard as an unidentified station. Every link uses these unless it is given something of its own, so they are stated once rather than repeated per link. Coordinates are decimal degrees, the same as a hotspot announces, and are optional — leave them out and this station is simply not plotted.

    Shown to other networks, not to your own members.

    Member passwords

    —

    Every member shares one password unless a directory is named here. With one, a member can be given their own, and removing that member is deleting one file — nobody else reconfigures anything. Without one, removing a member means a new shared password and every remaining member changing their hotspot on the same evening. Members without a password of their own keep using the shared one, so this can be adopted a member at a time. Passwords are issued on the access control page; the directory only says where they live.

    Where a member's own password is kept, if the club gives them one.

    What each member hears

    —

    Off, every member receives every talkgroup this network carries. That is right for a small club and wrong for a busy one: a hotspot has a single timeslot, so a member sitting on their local talkgroup cannot also take a statewide net. Turned on, a member hears a talkgroup once they transmit on it, and it lapses when they stop. Turn it on carefully — until somebody keys up they hear nothing, so a talkgroup everyone must always have needs pinning to each peer first, which is not yet editable here.

    Whether members receive every talkgroup, or only the ones they use.

    Call record

    —

    This is a log of members' activity — who transmitted, what they called, and when — readable by any administrator. It exists because a net control station uses the last-heard list to recover a check-in they missed, and fifty entries in memory survive neither the evening nor the deploy that follows it. Keeping nothing is a real answer for a club that would rather not hold one. No audio is stored: a record of who spoke is not a recording of what they said.

    How long a record of who transmitted is kept.

    Parrot

    —

    Program it as a group call. A private call is recorded and cannot be replayed usefully: DMR carries the call's addressing inside the audio burst, so a radio mutes a replay that is not addressed to it, and rewriting that means decoding the burst — which QSP deliberately does not do. The talkgroup must also be one the member's hotspot forwards; 9990 is outside the usual pass-through range and often needs a rule adding.

    Motorola repeaters

    —

    The master ID must not be a repeater's own ID. A repeater will not register with a master announcing the ID it uses itself, and it retries silently with nothing on either side saying why. QSP refuses a configuration that does it. The colour code must match the repeater's, and the slot bit setting decides which timeslot a transmission is understood to be on — if audio arrives and is not carried, that setting is the first thing to change.

    IP Site Connect carries Motorola repeaters, which speak a different protocol from hotspots and are configured separately. This was editable only by hand in qsp.json until now.

    Changing any of these needs a restart of QSP. The save says which settings, and what to run.
