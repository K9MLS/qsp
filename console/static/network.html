    Talkgroups members can use

    —

    Dialled is what goes into the radio; arrives is what this network sees. A hotspot running DMRGateway can rewrite a talkgroup number on its way out — a TGRewrite line in its configuration — so what a member enters is not always what QSP receives. When they match, leave arrives blank. When they do not and nobody has written it down, the symptom is a member who transmits into silence with every log looking healthy.

    Dialled is what goes into the radio; arrives is what this network sees. They differ when a hotspot rewrites the number on its way out, which is easy to configure and very hard to diagnose from either end. Leave arrives blank when they are the same.

    Parrot

    —

    Program it as a group call. A private call is recorded and cannot be replayed usefully: DMR carries the call's addressing inside the audio burst, so a radio mutes a replay that is not addressed to it, and rewriting that means decoding the burst — which QSP deliberately does not do. The talkgroup must also be one the member's hotspot forwards; 9990 is outside the usual pass-through range and often needs a rule adding.

    Plays a transmission back to whoever sent it, so a member can prove their radio, hotspot and network path all work without needing anybody else awake.

    Program it as a group call. A private call is recorded and cannot be replayed usefully: DMR carries the call's addressing inside the audio burst, so a radio mutes a replay that is not addressed to it, and rewriting that means decoding the burst — which QSP deliberately does not do.

    QSP swallows this number: nothing else on the network can use it while parrot is on. 9990 is the common convention.

    Changing parrot needs a restart of QSP; the save will say so.
